    Cooking with biogas on a Yemeni farm

    The study proposes a pilot in Hadramout. Its starting point is what one family can collect each day: 25 kg of fresh cattle manure and 25 litres of water. At this rate the farm needs about 9.13 tonnes of manure and 9.13 m³ of water each year.

    From daily feed to cooking fuel

    The design holds 40–60 days of slurry in a working volume of 2–3 m³, inside a nominal 3–4 m³ digester. Gas storage is sized separately for the family’s cooking schedule. At a design temperature of 35 °C, the target yield is 40 litres of biogas per kg of fresh manure. The study’s lower and upper cases, 15–50 litres/kg, give 0.375–1.25 m³/day. The IEA household benchmark is about 1 m³/day from 20–30 kg of manure.

    At the 1 m³/day target, the digester produces 365 m³/year. If the family uses 80% of that output to replace purchased fuel, it uses 292 m³/year. At 60% methane, this has the fuel energy of about 136.35 kg of LPG, assuming comparable stove efficiencies. The 80% describes gas used after production. The regional charts apply their 30% to manure collected before digestion.

    What the family could save

    The base case assumes a future installed price of US$400, US$25/year in cash operating costs, low-cost water and unpaid household labour. Savings depend on the price per kg of LPG actually bought. At US$0.65/kg, the family saves US$88.63/year in purchases, or US$63.63 after operating costs.

    At US$0.65/kg, a prototype estimated at US$550–900 takes 8.6–14.1 years to repay. For the US$400 case, the ten-year net present value at a 10% real discount rate is −US$9. Lower yield (25 litres/kg) extends simple payback to 13.2 years. Raising annual operating costs to US$70 at the target yield extends it to 21.5 years. The model excludes digestate revenue, financing and major replacements.

    What the sourcing evidence supports

    The Danish Refugee Council’s 2023 announcement records a Yemeni pilot with BioTreasure in Mukalla, a lead for installation and service. Art Plast’s catalogue covers liquid-handling components. Gas-contact parts require documented suitability for biogas.

    • Historical costs. A 2019 report from Yemen cited up to US$300 for a home digester. A 2024 Egyptian assessment reported US$800–1,500 for a household system.
    • Advertised prices. The study recorded US$60–80 for a 2 m³ PUXIN storage bag, minimum two, before freight.
    • Planning allowances. The study set aside US$15–35 for a stove. Its itemised engineering budget for the system totals US$425.50–793.50 including 15% contingency. The component allowances overlap this budget.

    The study found no dated quote for delivery and installation at a specific Yemeni farm. Before expansion, the proposed pilot would check dry-season water costs, weigh the feed, measure gas production and stove use separately, and compare actual LPG purchases. A qualified technician would review gas storage, ventilation and pressure protection.
